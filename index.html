NVIDIA (NVDA): Financial Statement Trends: Revenue, Segment Disaggregation, Margin Trends, and Capital Allocation Summary through Q1 FY2027

Aperture Insights · Published on July 29, 2026

NVIDIA is one of the world's most popular companies as its GPU chips enable the technology of the largest AI hyperscalers around the globe. The business model is focused on helping companies solve their computational challenges with their semiconductor processors and networking product offerings. Between the years ended January 28, 2024 and January 25, 2026, revenue more than tripled from $60.9 billion to $215.9 billion due to the growing demand within the economy for artificial intelligence. Nvidia's Blackwell GPUs have continued to account for the majority of its product shipments in the current fiscal year (2027). This page summarizes historical trends in revenue composition, margins, operating efficiency, and capital allocation based on Nvidia's quarterly filings between Q4 2024 and Q1 2027. Note that this is not a formal recommendation of the stock and does not constitute investment advice.

As reported in the 10-Q, the availability of data centers, energy and capital are essential for the buildout of the NVIDIA AI infrastructure. The Rubin platform is expected to begin shipping in the second half of 2027.

The global supply chain for networking products is driven by macroeconomic factors such as, inflation, tarriffs, inflation, interest rates, and volatility in capital markets.

Every series on this pages is based on the data as presented within NVIDIA’s SEC filings which have been pulled via the EDGAR XBRL company-facts and the corresponding segment footnote disclosure from each filing. NVIDIA reports on a fiscal year ending in late January, so FY2026 Q4 ≈ January 2026 and FY2027 Q1 ≈ April 2026. Fiscal-Q4 figures are derived as the full year less the three prior quarters, consistent across all statements.

Note: Beginning in Q1 FY 2027, the company redefined its reportable segments to AI Clouds, Industrial, and Enterprise (ACIE) and Hyperscale revenue within the Data Center revenue, and the second revenue segment being Edge computing.

Revenue composition & structural transition

The gross revenue table below reconciles to the related disclosure within the financial statements. Data Center revenue is based on the aggregation of the hyperscale revenue and ACIE revenue while the second reportable segment is edge computing. Prior to FY 20267, Data Center revenue was segmented into Compute and Networking with the remaining reportable segments being: Gaming, professional visualization, automotive, and OEM. Through FY24 Q4 – FY26 Q4 the five market platforms sum to the top line, with Data Center shown as Compute + Networking (“Compute Data Center”). At Q1 FY27 the disclosure shifts: AICIE + Hyperscale re-form Data Center, and Edge Computing reconciles the remainder. Toggle to Share (%) to read mix rather than level.

Commentary

As disclosed within the 10-Q, revenue growth was largely driven by the increase in the Blackwell 300 product sells and the increased demand for the InfiniBand, Spectrum-X ethernet, and NVLink solutions. largely driven by one line item, as reported via the Q1 2027 earnings: Data Center revenue which increased from $18.4B (83% of revenue) in FY24 Q4 to $75.2B (92%) in FY27 Q1. As noted during that call, the new Blackwell 300 products and demand for InfiniBand, Spectrum-XTM Ethernet, and NVLinkTM solutions were the key drivers of data center revenue in Q1 2027. Gaming, Professional Visualization, Automotive, and OEM weren't eliminated — under the recast presentation NVIDIA folded them into Edge Computing ($6.4B in FY27 Q1, up 29% YoY). They tie out exactly: Gaming + Pro Viz + Automotive + OEM = Edge Computing ($5.81B in FY26 Q4; $4.95B in FY26 Q1), while Data Center carries through unchanged. The FY27 Q1 revenue includes AICIE ($37.4B) + Hyperscale ($37.9B) = Data Center ($75.2B), plus Edge Computing ($6.4B) which is a reporting change in how one segment is cut; however, it is not a change in the business.

Note on comparability. The pre- and post-transition members do not co-exist in any single quarter, so they occupy different columns by design. “Compute Data Center” is the ASC 280 Compute + Networking view; AICIE / Hyperscale / Edge Computing are the ASC 606 disaggregation. Reading the trajectory within a regime is more reliable than splicing a level across the FY27 Q1 boundary.

Panel A — Margin inflection points

Gross margin troughs at 60.5% in FY26 Q1 on inventory due to government imposed export control licensing requirements on NVIDIA's H20 products for the China market. The margin recovers to 75.0% by FY26 Q4 and holds through the segment transition. Operating margin expands to a period high on scale; net margin in FY27 Q1 is lifted by non-operating equity gains and reads above the operating line.

Commentary

The gross-margin path is the clearest signal in the set: a sharp, identifiable dislocation in FY26 Q1 followed by a clean climb back to the mid-70s. Because the estimated non-GAAP line sits only ~30–50bps above GAAP, the two move together — the story is the recovery, not the GAAP/non-GAAP wedge.

Read operating margin, not net. FY27 Q1 net margin (71.5%) exceeds operating margin (65.6%) because ~$15.9B of non-operating equity-security gains flow below the operating line. Operating margin is the truer efficiency read; it, not net income, reflects the scaling of the core business.

Panel B — Operating efficiency indicators

Operating expense steps up every quarter but grows far slower than revenue — the operating-leverage engine. The FY27 Q1 step (R&D $6.32B, +$0.81B QoQ) is the largest single-quarter build in the window, which NVIDIA's MD&A attributes to higher compensation and benefits from employee growth, compute and infrastructure costs, and engineering development materials for new product developments. The FY27 Q1 step (R&D $6.32B, +$0.81B QoQ) is the largest single-quarter build in the window, consistent with the engineering / overhead load accompanying the new-segment rollout.

Commentary

Total operating expense rises from $3.18B (FY24 Q4) to $7.62B (FY27 Q1), or more than double; while revenue rose 3.7×. That gap is why operating expense as a share of revenue falls from ~14% to ~9% across the window even as the absolute build accelerates. R&D is the primary driver of all of the growth given that it accounts for more than 80% of total operating expenses while SG&A grew far more slowly (up ~83% over the window versus R&D's ~2.6×), falling sharply as a share of revenue.

Commentary

NVIDIA converts almost all operating cash into free cash flow — CapEx stays near $1–2B a quarter even as OCF scales past $50B, so free cash flow tracks operating cash closely (FY27 Q1 conversion ~96%). The capital-light model leaves the cash to accumulate and to fund returns.

Buybacks dominate the return mix; the dividend is a token ~$0.24B per quarter. Repurchases are lumpy — a $19.3B FY27 Q1 quarter against $3.8B the quarter before — but the cumulative line is a steady climb to $97.9B returned over the ten quarters. Because buybacks shrink the share count, they support diluted EPS independent of net income.
